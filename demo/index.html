Cable assemblies are built from a bill of materials that lists every wire, connector, terminal and seal. Each part has its own lead time, minimum order quantity and price break, and the quote for a harness depends on all of them at once. Labour is estimated from the number of cut, strip, crimp and test operations, and from how much of that work can be done on automatic machines.

When a customer changes a drawing, the quote has to change with it. A new connector can mean a new crimp tool, a longer lead time, or a different supplier, and the effect ripples through the delivery date and the margin. Keeping a clear history of revisions makes it much easier to explain why a price moved.

Testing is part of the cost too. Continuity and insulation tests are routine, but some customers need hipot testing, pull tests on crimps, or full traceability of every lot. Those requirements belong in the quote from the start, not as surprises on the invoice.
